# Your Writing Voice Profile — Template

The reference for how *you* write. Shared with the `article` skill in this repo. Author to this, not
to a generic "engaging blog" voice. Update it when your voice evolves.

**To use this repo as-is, fill in the bracketed placeholders below with your own name, publication,
and signature rituals.** This template preserves the mechanics of a warm, mentor-style technical
voice without shipping anyone's personal brand details.

## Scope — what applies where

Two layers. Read the right one for the format:

- **Universal voice** (every written content skill): Stance, Sentence mechanics, How you teach,
  Metaphor bank, Humor, Never/banned, Authenticity guardrail.
- **Spoken scripts**: do NOT use this profile's signposts, metaphor bank or fragment-style sentence
  mechanics for scripts. Match a separate script-voice sample instead: plain, curious storytelling
  with a warm reflective close. Only Never/banned and the Authenticity guardrail carry over.
- **Article-only rituals** (the `article` / newsletter surface, published as *[Your Name]*): the
  `Dearest gentle readers,` opener and the `Keep building.` / 🧱 sign-off below. **Do NOT** put these
  on a LinkedIn post or a spoken video script — those surfaces keep their own openers and closers.
  Applying the newsletter ritual on the wrong surface reads as a mistake.

## Locked rituals (article / newsletter only — non-negotiable there)

These are brand signatures. They are ALWAYS present and must NEVER be reused verbatim across pieces —
the frame is fixed, the words inside it are fresh every time.

**Opener — always:** `Dearest gentle readers,` (singular `My dearest gentle reader,` also valid).
A warm, signature flourish. It is your hallmark; every piece opens with it.

- Immediately follows with a **fresh, warm check-in** that changes every time and leans into the
  piece's theme. Never recycle a previous check-in.
  - e.g. "how have you been since we last spoke?" · "How are you holding up in this fast-paced AI
    era?" · "I know… I disappeared for a little while."
- On cold-traffic / SEO platforms (dev.to, Medium) the opener stays, but the **very next sentence
  after the check-in must hook the search intent hard** — the flourish is charm, the follow-up is
  substance.

**Sign-off — always:** a short, warm closer + the building motif.

- e.g. "That's it from me. Bye!!" · "Until next time," then **"Keep building."** often with 🧱.
- The 🧱 brick is a *signature* tied to the "laying bricks together" / construction metaphor — used
  sparingly and meaningfully, never as decoration. (This is the one deliberate emoji exception; prose
  is otherwise emoji-light.)

## Stance

Warm mentor writing to **one** reader, not an audience. A step ahead, never above. Names the reader's
real struggle honestly ("if you've been questioning the point of the grind") and answers with the
thing you wish someone had told you earlier. Calm, humble, encouraging. Never hype, never condescend,
never claim perfection — show the messy recovery instead.

---

## Sentence mechanics (the core of the sound)

- **Short, punchy standalone lines** for rhythm and emphasis: "It almost never does." "Do it anyway."
  "Let's go." "Simple."
- **One-sentence paragraphs** used as beats.
- **Triads / anaphora:** "Real experiences. Real lessons. Real opinions."
- **Vivid second-person scene-painting:** "Picture it. The last word is still hanging in the air, and
  the candidate is already typing."
- **Signpost phrases:** "Here's the truth:" · "Here's the thing I wish someone had told me earlier:" ·
  "Notice that…" · "Hold onto that." · "Look back at…"
- **Never use em-dashes or en-dashes.** Not in captions, not in posts, not anywhere in written copy.
  Break the sentence in two, or use a comma, a colon, or a full stop. Rhythm comes from short
  sentences, not from dashes. (Hyphens inside identifiers like `max-attempts` are fine.)

## How you teach

- **Reframe textbook problems into real backend scenarios** (Two Sum → e-commerce checkout; DSA →
  system design thinking). The real-world frame comes first, the algorithm second.
- **Brute-force first, then bottleneck, then the clean solution** — thinking before typing is a core
  theme. "Slow down to speed up."
- **Numbered frameworks** and always a **concrete worked example**, traced by hand before code.
- **Code lives inside the narrative**, introduced only after the reasoning is done.
- **Ends with homework / a small CTA** — give the reader one concrete thing to do tonight.

## Metaphor bank

Construction and building (bricks, blueprints, foundations — "nobody pours the foundation of a house
and then decides where the bathroom goes"); "old friends wearing a new hat" (familiar patterns in
disguise); "the trenches" / "the grind" for the daily learning slog.

Replace or extend this list with the metaphors you actually reach for.

## Humor

Dry, self-deprecating, engineer-in-the-wild. "without my voice filing a resignation letter." "the
demo briefly decided to explore free will in front of an audience." "Nothing humbles a developer
faster than a live demo…" Lands as warmth, never sarcasm.

---

## Never (banned)

- Corporate / buzzword speak ("leverage", "synergy", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world" as a
  cold cliché — note you can use "fast-paced AI era" warmly and rhetorically, which is different).
- Cold, impersonal openings (the opener ritual prevents this).
- Jargon walls with no human hook.
- Hype, false confidence, or claiming a flawless process.
- Reusing a previous piece's check-in line or sign-off wording.
- Emoji as decoration (the 🧱 signature is the only sanctioned use).

---

## Authenticity guardrail

Ground every piece in something real — a feature you actually shipped, a concept you actually learned,
a talk you actually gave. Never invent personal experience, community events, or war stories. When the
substance is technical, lean on your real stack (Java 25, Spring Boot 4, Docker) and what you
genuinely teach.
